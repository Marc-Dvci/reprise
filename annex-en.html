01 / Biomimicry as a design method

The biological transfer is selective renewal: restore a worn function without replacing every supporting component. Circular use is the intended outcome; biology supplies a specific rule that can be translated and tested. Materials, energy and maintenance remain external inputs.

Selective renewal

Epidermal renewal maintains a barrier through local replacement of cells.

Service the optical layer before replacing a complete reflector. Recoat sound film, replace damaged strips and inspect the retained frame. Removable cartridges, access lanes and repair benches make this rule spatial and operational.

Book pp. 2, 7 and 12. Measure retained mass, replacement time, accepted reflectance and crew hours over repeated renewals.

Periodic surface renewal

Arthropod moulting sheds an old cuticle and enables growth beneath a newly formed one.

Two replaceable 2.5 m strips attach to a 5 × 10 m reusable frame. The transfer concerns periodic renewal and a vulnerable transition, not a fixed biological skeleton: the reflector must be stowed during exchange and optically accepted before returning to service.

Book p. 3. Compare repeated strip exchange against complete replacement using the same frame, receiver and exposure history.

Mesa et al., 2018 · Arthropod moulting, 2024

02 / The plan follows the work

Two atmospheres, one service interface

Coating operates inside local hoods in unpressurised courts; people work inside a pressure shell. A contained transfer threshold connects these environments. This puts diagnosis close to the machinery without pressurising the full industrial volume.

Separate material and crew journeys

The B1–B2 material circuit and the rover entrance serve different cleanliness needs. Their separation limits routine crossings; quarantine, cleaning and acceptance are required before any part enters the human workshop. Separation alone cannot stop electrostatic or ballistic dust.

A shared logistics street

The three courts open onto a covered transfer street for rolls, cartridges and maintenance equipment. The 2.5 m web needs controlled alignment inside the coating line; rolled stock can turn during transport. Straight service access is a design choice, not proof that every court must form one continuous web.

A shield that can outlast its contents

The regolith shield rests on its own ribs and ties beside the production courts. Pressure modules can be installed and serviced independently of the shielding load. Radiator banks need surveyed views and contamination setbacks. Soil, access and replacement clearances must still be verified.

The proposal combines short service paths, independent lifetimes and human proximity. It is a defensible arrangement, not a demonstrated unique optimum. A compact pressure factory would add pressurised volume; a dispersed layout would lengthen transfers. Those alternatives require a measured comparison before claiming a quantified advantage.

03 / Ten interfaces to qualify

Open an interface for its design basis and acceptance path. P0-A–F: terrestrial work; P1: hosted lunar test; P2: factory procurement; P3: occupied operation.

01 / Abrasive and electrostatic dust reaching coating and mechanisms P0-B / P0-C / P1

Current design

Material and crew routes are separated end to end: a one-way circuit through gates B1 and B2 for material, a rover dock and contained cleaning threshold for people, exchangeable filtration and sealed equipment lockers at the entry. Deposition is inhibited during nearby landings, venting, dust-gate failure or unknown contamination.

Evidence required

A hosted robotic experiment in the lunar environment, observing dust behaviour, vacuum operation, thermal operation and the maintenance interfaces before any P2 procurement. First test charged simulant under vacuum at seals, bearings and transfer gates; measure leakage, wear, torque and post-cleaning residue. Contained capture is required: open brushing can redistribute dust. Electrodynamic cleaning is a candidate for exposed surfaces, subject to optical and electrical compatibility tests; it is not an assumed qualified component.

02 / Contamination around aluminium evaporation P0-B

Current design

Twelve evaporation sources under a hood, with a capture target of at least 99% of material that misses the film. Capture percentage alone is not treated as a safe radius.

Evidence required

Weigh source charge, product deposit and collected or escaped material; witness surfaces and residual-gas measurement; start, stop and cartridge-exchange tests, with a closed measurement uncertainty budget. No lunar release until a contamination allocation for neighbours is met.

03 / Optical tolerance and pointing after repeated renewal P0-A, P0-C

Current design

100 nm ±10% thickness as a trial target; solar-weighted reflectance at least 0.85 and within 0.02 of a paired Earth-coated control; installed receiver interception at least 95% of that control under the same geometry. Final optical tolerance comes from the customer, not from the project.

Evidence required

Coupons and a ≥250 mm moving web over at least three independent runs, then a full 5 × 10 m mechanically joined module measuring pretension, sag, rail displacement, joint slip, tear propagation and replacement time across repeated strip exchanges. The full-module acceptance also repeats pointing calibration, slope/shape mapping and receiver interception after each renewal and thermal exposure; thickness alone cannot establish optical quality.

04 / Anchoring, foundations and construction logistics P0-E

Current design

Maximum factored footing force 77.36 kN, or 25.12 kPa over a 1.4 × 2.2 m shoe, against a nominal ultimate bearing target of at least 90 kPa. Maximum tie reaction 33.79 kN, 42.24 MPa in the selected tie area. Sequence: survey and proof-load footings, install ties and braced rib segments, fit bearers and plates, fill symmetrically in controlled lifts, verify settlement and shape, then commission the pressure package.

Evidence required

A full-size rib, connection and retaining-panel load article with representative soil-footing tests, including staged and asymmetric fill, correlated against a nonlinear analysis. The reported Euler result covers an ideal 1.5 m segment and does not establish global arch stability.

05 / Off-loading and placing 79.66 t of equipment P1, P2

Current design

17.30 t P2 equipment, 51.86 t P3 crew spaces including 30% growth, 10.50 t solar, utility and rover allowance. At 12–15 t per load this is 6–7 loads by mass only; excavation fleet, landers and launch integration are excluded and named as excluded.

Evidence required

The load count is a planning illustration against published cargo planning, not a delivery service or a booked manifest. Placement and offloading are scoped with the delivery operator before P2 procurement. Freeze package dimensions, centres of gravity, lifting points, transport restraints and a mass/volume manifest. Rehearse crane or ramp offloading, rover reach and placement on a terrain mock-up; establish safe slopes and a failed-lift recovery procedure. No crane capacity or lander compatibility is asserted yet.

06 / Long-duration thermal cycling P0-C

Current design

A 200 K excursion at 20 ppm/K gives about 40 mm of film movement over 10 m; 80 mm of tensioner travel is allocated against it. Gravitational sag screens at 14.49 mm at 50 N/m. These are compliance allowances, not a qualified cycle life.

Evidence required

At least 100 hot and cold cycles across a mission-approved envelope as a first screening stage, stated explicitly as a screen and not as ten-year qualification. Seams, pretension tolerance, rail motion, wrinkles and thermal gradients need the full module test.

07 / Pressure-module detail and atmosphere P3

Current design

Five 6 m modules give a 30 m pressure street and about 135 m² gross floor at 4.5 m clear nominal width. Design atmosphere 70 kPa at 30% oxygen, inspired oxygen 19.12 kPa after water-vapour correction. This is a selected atmosphere, not an approved one.

Evidence required

Weld, hatch, proof-pressure, leak and fatigue qualification of the pressure package; material flammability and decompression/EVA compatibility tested at this oxygen fraction; trace contaminants, particulate control, noise and personal exposure instrumented.

08 / Radiation dose and impact P3

Current design

A 2.5 m crown allowance over a 12 m span, 5.9 m inner and 8.4 m outer crown. Crown depth is not described as a constant thickness around every occupied point, and regolith depth alone is not treated as establishing an acceptable dose.

Evidence required

Particle transport through the actual geometry, secondary radiation, hatch and penetration streaming, solar-event refuge performance, and shell and shielding impact resistance. Visually protected spaces are not described as certified safe havens.

09 / Operational interface with the settlement supplying the crew P3

Current design

Consumables are stocked from Earth or the base; condensate and waste return there. Crew entry requires verified consumables, reserve energy, a return route, radiation clearance and an independently powered pressurised rover already docked. The rover is a rescue resource, not a substitute for module stocks.

Evidence required

Agreed settlement rooms, rover access and emergency arrangements with the intended operator, with shared services retaining their costs. Occupied operations require the operator’s acceptance; an architectural render does not close this gate.

10 / Whether local coating is worth its own hardware P0-F

Current design

A 100 nm aluminium layer over the ten-year 30,000 m² scenario is 8.1 kg. New-film metallisation alone cannot justify 17.3 t of equipment, and the book says so on the page that presents the economics.

Evidence required

Compare delivered hardware, replacement film, accepted area, energy, crew time, downtime and retrieval under both the local-renewal and the Earth-coated route, with demand and failure sensitivity. Proceed only if an identified operator sees a favourable complete service comparison.

04 / Operating envelopes and refuge

These scenarios use the existing calculation ledger. Entry remains conditional on stocks, independently powered docked rover, return route and operator release. An eight-hour visit is not a permanently inhabited base.

Six battery cabinets: 216 kWh usable, 43.2 h at 5 kW; reference darkness 39 h.

One cabinet lost: 180 kWh, only 36 h at 5 kW. A proposed unoccupied 4 kW mode gives 45 h, subject to thermal validation.

Four radiator banks: 51.2 kW rejection for a 49 kW load. One bank lost: cap production at 20 kW; 38.4 kW rejection for 36.4 kW load. Two banks lost: stop production.

05 / The first Earth experiment

Compare Earth-coated film after transport exposures, film coated after those exposures, and renewal of retained film. Use three independent batches, three packaging levels and the same substrate and optical acceptance. The €38.6k estimate covers coupons and packaging, not a 2.5 m coating line, full hood, lunar deployment or an occupied module.

  1. Months 0–2: agree protocol, facility access and paired controls.
  2. Months 2–5: coupon deposition, packaging exposures and capture measurements.
  3. Months 5–8: follow-on web and module trials, with separate funding.
  4. Months 8–10: independent review and service-value decision, conditional on facility access.
